Gas Line Installation & Repair in Costa Mesa, CA

Gas line repair fixes damaged or leaking fuel gas piping on your side of the meter. Installation adds new lines for ranges, dryers, water heaters, fireplaces, pool heaters and outdoor kitchens. In Costa Mesa, CA, gas work is permitted through the city and pressure-tested before the gas is turned back on.

Do not call a plumber first. Do this, in this order.

New gas lines and upsizing

Adding an appliance is often more than running a new branch. Gas piping is sized for the total load of everything connected to it. A tankless water heater, a large range or a pool heater can push an older system past what its pipes can carry.

We calculate the load against the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and includes the fuel gas sizing tables. If the existing line is too small, we upsize it from the meter. This comes up often with tankless water heater installs.

Before any buried line is dug, underground utilities are marked through the 811 call-before-you-dig system.

Earthquakes and gas connections

Rigid connections at appliances can crack when the ground moves. Flexible, code-approved connectors and a properly braced water heater reduce that risk.

Some homeowners also add an automatic seismic gas shutoff valve at the meter. It is optional for most existing homes, and it must be a state-certified model installed with a permit. We can explain the trade-offs, including that a tripped valve needs relighting of every pilot.

Old uncoated brass appliance connectors should be replaced. They were never designed to last as long as some are still in use.

Appliance hookups we handle

Each hookup includes a new code-approved connector, a shutoff valve at the appliance and a leak test.

  • Gas ranges and cooktops, including kitchen remodels that move the range.
  • Gas dryers, which need a dedicated shutoff and proper venting clearance.
  • Water heaters, tank or tankless.
  • Fireplace inserts and log sets.
  • Barbecues, fire pits and outdoor kitchens, usually with a buried line.
  • Pool and spa heaters, which draw a large load.

Converting an electric appliance to gas, or the other way, often means new piping or a new circuit. We plan both before quoting.

Gas problems that are not emergencies

Some gas issues are worth a call but not an evacuation. A yellow or lazy flame on a range burner usually points to the appliance or its air adjustment. A pilot that keeps going out on a water heater is often a thermocouple or flame sensor.

An old appliance connector is the one to act on soon. Flexible connectors wear out, and older uncoated brass connectors should be replaced whenever an appliance is moved or serviced. If you ever smell gas, treat it as an emergency first.
