Sewer Camera Inspection in Costa Mesa, CA

A sewer camera inspection is a recorded video of your sewer lateral. A waterproof camera on a flexible cable travels from the house to the public main and shows the condition of every joint. In Costa Mesa, CA, it matters twice over. It shows what a repair really needs, and the Costa Mesa Sanitary District pays part of the cost when it is recorded to their standard.

The CMSD rebate, in plain terms

The Costa Mesa Sanitary District runs a rebate program for residents who inspect their laterals. The amounts depend on how the camera gets into the line.

Sewer Inspection Rebate amounts
WorkRebateCondition
Camera run from a ground-level cleanout.Up to $200.Must record to past the main connection.
Camera run from a roof vent or toilet flange.Up to $250.Same recording rules apply.
Installing a ground-level cleanout.Up to $500.Must be permitted.

Apply with the district before the work and get a confirmation number. The approval stays valid for three months after that, so schedule the inspection inside that window.

What the video has to show to qualify

Rebate footage that misses one of these can be rejected, so we set the camera up for it from the start.

  • Your property address on screen.
  • The date of the inspection on screen.
  • A footage counter that runs the whole time.
  • The entire lateral from the house until past the connection to the district main.

What a camera run tells you

The footage shows the pipe material, the joints, and anything inside the line. Common findings in older Costa Mesa homes include root intrusion at clay joints, cracked or offset sections, heavy grease buildup, and low spots where water sits.

It also shows distance. The footage counter tells us exactly how far from the entry point a problem sits, and a locator on the camera head tells us how deep it is. That turns a vague repair into a precise one.

Repair quotes for the lateral are built from this footage, and so are replacement quotes.

A camera run during escrow is worth ordering in Mesa Verde, the Eastside and Halecrest, since the original laterals in those 1950s and 1960s neighborhoods are rarely documented.

Buying a home built in the 1950s or 1960s? A camera inspection before closing costs little next to a surprise lateral replacement a year later.

Pipe materials you may see on the footage

The material tells you a lot about how the line will age from here.

Common lateral materials
MaterialHow to recognize itWhat it means
Vitrified clayShort sections with a joint every few feet.Durable pipe, but roots enter at joints.
Cast ironDark, often scaly interior.Rust and scale narrow the pipe over decades.
ABS or PVC plasticSmooth black or white interior, long sections.Newer pipe, usually from a past repair.
Mixed materialsA change in color or texture mid-run.An earlier repair. Check the transition joints.

Many older laterals are a patchwork. The footage shows exactly where one material ends and the next begins, which is where problems often start.

How an inspection visit runs

  1. Access. We open the cleanout, or use a roof vent or toilet flange if there is no cleanout.
  2. Clear if needed. A blocked line gets cleared first so the camera can pass.
  3. Record. The camera travels the full lateral with the address, date and counter on screen.
  4. Review. We walk you through the footage and mark anything that needs attention.

If the line is blocked when we arrive, clearing the clog comes first, and the camera run follows on the same visit.
