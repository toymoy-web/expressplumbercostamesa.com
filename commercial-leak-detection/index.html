Commercial Leak Detection in Costa Mesa, CA

Already narrowed it down? Good

Building engineers and managers often call having done half the work. They know the leak is somewhere in one hallway or under one section of floor, but they do not have the equipment to find the exact spot.

That head start saves time. We start where your search stopped, isolate that section, and use acoustic listening, thermal imaging and pressure testing to mark the leak within a small area before anything is cut.

How a commercial test runs

  1. Walkthrough. Review drawings if available, locate shutoffs, meters and sub-meters.
  2. Schedule the shutdown. Pick a window that keeps the business open.
  3. Isolate zones. Close valves section by section and watch for the loss.
  4. Pinpoint. Acoustic, thermal and pressure tools narrow it to a small area.
  5. Report. Written findings, photos and a repair quote for whoever approves the spend.

Pressure testing follows the same acceptance method the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), uses for new piping.

Who pays: landlord, tenant or association

In commercial and multi-unit buildings, the leak's location often decides who pays. A line serving one tenant's space may be the tenant's under the lease. A shared riser or service line is usually the owner's or the association's.

Written findings that every party can see settle that question faster than any argument. We address the report to whoever you name and include exact locations and photos.

Keep the report with your maintenance records. Insurers commonly ask for it when a claim follows.

Leaks that come with other obligations

Some commercial leaks sit next to regulated equipment. Food businesses in Costa Mesa hold wastewater discharge permits under the sanitary district's grease control program, so work near grease lines is planned with that in mind. Properties with backflow assemblies need them retested if a repair disturbs them, since Mesa Water requires annual testing.

For the repairs and maintenance that follow, see our commercial plumbing service. Homes use the residential leak detection process instead.

In Costa Mesa that means office towers and retail around South Coast Plaza and South Coast Metro, restaurants along Harbor Boulevard and 17th Street, and the creative offices and shops of the SoBECA district on Bristol Street.

Signs a commercial building is losing water

Buildings rarely announce a leak with a puddle. These are the quieter signals.

  • A water bill that climbs while occupancy and hours stay the same.
  • A main meter that keeps moving overnight when the building is closed.
  • Ceiling tiles with rings or sagging below restrooms or break rooms.
  • Warm spots on a slab floor, or a musty smell in one suite.
  • Pumps or booster systems cycling more often than usual.

Comparing the main meter with any tenant sub-meters is often the fastest way to see which part of the building the water is going to.
