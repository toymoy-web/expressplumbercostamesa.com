Toilet Repair & Installation in Costa Mesa, CA

Toilet repair fixes leaks, constant running, weak flushes and rocking bowls. Installation replaces the whole fixture. In Costa Mesa, CA, the most common call is a toilet that runs all night, and the cause is often a rubber part worn out faster than expected.

A toilet leaking at the base should not wait. Water seeps into the subfloor long before it shows on the ceiling below.

Why flappers wear out fast here

Mesa Water disinfects its water with chloramines. Chloramines are known to break down some types of rubber, and the toilet flapper is the part most exposed to it. A soft, warped flapper lets water trickle into the bowl, and the tank refills around the clock.

When we replace a flapper, we use one rated for chloramine. It costs a little more and usually lasts much longer.

A quick test: put a few drops of food coloring in the tank. If color shows in the bowl within 15 minutes without flushing, the flapper is leaking.

Replacing an old toilet

California's appliance standards limit new toilets to 1.28 gallons per flush. Many toilets in 1950s and 1960s homes were built to use far more. A state law on water-conserving fixtures also requires older, noncompliant fixtures to be replaced when a home is altered or improved, so a remodel is a natural time to swap them.

Modern low-flow toilets flush well when the drain line is in good shape. If an old line is partly blocked, a new toilet can seem weak. We check the drain before blaming the fixture.

Rough-in distance matters. Most toilets fit a 12-inch rough-in from the wall, but some older bathrooms use 10 or 14 inches. We measure before you buy.

What to do before we arrive

  • Overflowing bowl: lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, then turn the valve behind the toilet clockwise.
  • Several fixtures backing up at once: stop using water. That is a main line clog, not the toilet.
  • Leak at the base: stop using that toilet and put towels down to protect the floor.

Main line trouble is handled through main line clearing.

Toilet clogs that keep coming back

One clog is usually something that should not have been flushed. A toilet that clogs every week is a different problem. The cause is often in the drain beyond the toilet, such as buildup in an old cast iron line or a partial blockage in the main.

A closet auger clears the toilet itself without scratching the bowl. If other drains in the house are slow too, or the toilet gurgles when the washer drains, the main line needs attention.

Low-flow toilets need a drain in good shape to carry waste away. When an older line has buildup or a belly, a new 1.28 gallon toilet can seem to clog more than the old one did.
