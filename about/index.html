ABOUT · Costa Mesa, CA

About Costa Mesa Express Plumbing

What this site is

Costa Mesa Express Plumbing is a 24/7 plumbing line and information site for Costa Mesa, CA and five nearby cities. When you call (714) 750-8637, a person answers, asks what is happening, and connects the job to a plumber who serves your address.

The pages on this site exist to help you understand a problem before and after you call: who owns which pipe, what the local rebates are, what a repair usually involves, and what moves the price.

How we write about Costa Mesa

Local details on this site come from public sources: the Costa Mesa Sanitary District, Mesa Water District and the other utilities and cities we serve, plus the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Where a detail varies by address, we say so and tell you where to confirm it.

Water hardness, rebate amounts and rules change over time. The facts on these pages were last checked in September 2026. If you find something out of date, tell us when you call.

What we will not claim

You will not find invented reviews, star ratings, years-in-business figures or badges on this site. We do not make health claims about water. We do not promise arrival times we cannot keep.

California requires plumbing contractors to hold a CSLB license for work above the state's minor-work threshold. Before work starts, you can check any contractor's license at cslb.ca.gov. Plumbing falls under the C-36 classification.

What happens when you call

The call is answered by a person, not a menu. The first questions are the address and what you are seeing, because those two answers decide how urgent the job is and which plumber is closest.

If water is running, the dispatcher walks you to the right shutoff before anything else. If sewage is backing up, you are told to stop using water in the house. If you smell gas, you are told to leave and call SoCalGas first. Only then does the conversation turn to scheduling.

You are given an honest arrival estimate for your address. If it changes, you hear about it.

What to do before we arrive

Three steps cover most emergencies. For running water, close the house shutoff where the supply line enters the home. For a sewage backup, stop using every fixture. For a gas smell, leave the building and call SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 or 911 from outside. Then take photos of any damage before cleanup starts, since insurers usually ask for them.

The agencies behind Costa Mesa plumbing

Most plumbing problems in Costa Mesa involve at least one public agency, and knowing which one saves time. Mesa Water District or the Irvine Ranch Water District supplies the water, depending on the address. The Costa Mesa Sanitary District owns the public sewer mains and runs the sewer inspection rebate. The City of Costa Mesa issues plumbing permits and street encroachment permits. SoCalGas owns the gas meter and handles gas emergencies.

Pages on this site explain where each agency's responsibility ends and the property owner's begins, because that line decides who pays for a repair.

The homes most common here

Costa Mesa incorporated in 1953, and much of its housing went up in the decade after. Many calls come from single-story ranch homes on the Eastside and Westside, mid-century tracts in Mesa Verde, and condos built in the 1970s and 1980s.

Homes of that age tend to have original galvanized or early copper supply lines, clay or cast iron sewer laterals and water heaters in garages that predate current bracing rules. That shapes what we write about and what the dispatcher asks.

Renters, landlords and property managers

A good share of calls come from rental homes and condos. When a tenant calls, we ask for the owner or property manager's contact so the right person approves the work and receives the invoice. Most repairs to supply lines, sewer laterals and water heaters are the owner's responsibility, and we route the quote accordingly.

For condos and townhomes, the first step is often working out whether a leak comes from your unit, a neighbor or a shared line, because the association's rules decide who pays.

How pricing works

The price for a job is given before the work starts. Planned work such as a water heater replacement or a repipe gets a free estimate. Emergency calls get a written price once the problem is diagnosed, so you can decide before anything is repaired.

Work that needs a permit, such as a water heater replacement, a repipe or a sewer lateral repair, is permitted with the right city and inspected before it is closed up.
