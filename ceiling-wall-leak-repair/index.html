Ceiling and Wall Leak Repair in Costa Mesa, CA

Ceiling and wall leak repair traces water on drywall back to the pipe or fixture behind it. Then we fix that source through the smallest opening possible. In Costa Mesa, CA, the source is often a bathroom above, a supply line inside the wall, or a condo neighbor's plumbing.

What to do when water is coming through

  • Stop using the bathroom or fixtures directly above the stain.
  • If water is dripping steadily, close the house shutoff or the unit shutoff.
  • Put a bucket under the drip. If the ceiling is sagging, poke a small hole at the lowest point to let it drain.
  • Turn off lights and power in that area if water is near fixtures.
  • In a condo, call the association's emergency line and the neighbor above.

A sagging, water-filled ceiling can come down without warning. Keep people out from under it.

Plumbing or roof?

The timing of the stain is the best clue to whether you need a plumber at all.

We do not repair roofs. If the evidence points to the roof, we will say so and you avoid paying for plumbing work you do not need.

The usual sources above a stain

Under a bathroom, the most common culprit is a toilet wax ring that no longer seals, which leaks a little with every flush. Next come shower valves and tub drains, where a worn seal or cracked joint lets water into the floor below.

Inside walls, supply lines and their fittings fail with age, especially in older homes with galvanized pipe or early copper. A slow drip can soak insulation and drywall for weeks before a stain shows.

If the leak turns out to be a supply line with no obvious fixture above it, our non-invasive leak testing pinpoints it before any wall is opened.

How we trace it with minimal damage

  1. Map the moisture. Meters show where the drywall is wettest and how far it spread.
  2. Test the fixtures above. Run the shower, flush the toilet and fill the tub one at a time.
  3. Use thermal imaging. Hot water leaks show up through walls and ceilings.
  4. Open small. Cut one clean, square opening at the source, easy to patch.
  5. Repair and test. Fix the pipe or seal and confirm it stays dry under use.

Repairs follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

Condos and townhomes

In a condo, the water on your ceiling may come from the unit above, a shared line, or your own plumbing. The source usually decides who pays, and your association's governing documents set the details.

Written findings help every party. We document where the water came from and what was repaired, so the owner, neighbor and association work from the same facts.
