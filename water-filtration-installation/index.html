Water Filtration Installation in Costa Mesa, CA

Water filtration installation adds a system that removes taste, odor and specific contaminants from your water, either for the whole house or at one tap. In Costa Mesa, CA, the water already meets state and federal standards, so filtration is about the specific thing you want to change, most often chloramine taste.

Start with what is actually in your water

Mesa Water publishes an annual Water Quality Report with test results for every regulated and many unregulated constituents. Its 2025 report confirms the supply meets all state and federal drinking water standards. IRWD customers get a separate report.

Reading it first keeps you from buying a system for a problem you do not have. We make no health claims about specific contaminants. If you have a health concern, talk to your doctor and your utility.

Chloramine and why the filter type matters

Mesa Water disinfects with chloramines, a mix of chlorine and ammonia that lasts longer in pipes than chlorine alone. That stability is good for the water system, but it also means ordinary carbon filters remove it more slowly.

Catalytic carbon is designed for chloramine. For a whole-house system, it is the media to ask for. Fish tanks and some medical equipment need chloramine removed completely, and those uses need specific treatment beyond household filtration.

Chloramine can also wear rubber seals in fixtures. Filtration at the main line reduces that effect for the whole house.

What to do to keep a filter system working

  • Change carbon and sediment cartridges on the schedule on the housing label, not when taste changes.
  • Replace reverse osmosis membranes every few years, or when output drops.
  • Check under-sink units for drips at the fittings after every filter change.
  • Sanitize the RO storage tank when you replace the membrane.

Under-sink or whole-house

An under-sink system treats only the water you drink and cook with. It costs less, fits in most kitchens, and filter changes take minutes.

A whole-house system treats every tap and shower. It suits households bothered by chloramine smell in the shower, or anyone who wants the same water everywhere. It needs space near the main line, a bypass valve for servicing, and larger media tanks or cartridges.

Many homes end up with both: a catalytic carbon system at the main line and a reverse osmosis unit at the kitchen sink for drinking water.

Reading a filter's certification

Independent certification tells you what a filter actually does. NSF/ANSI Standard 42 covers aesthetic effects such as chlorine and chloramine taste and odor. Standard 53 covers specific health-related contaminants. Standard 58 applies to reverse osmosis systems.

Check the certification listing for the exact claim you care about. A filter certified under Standard 42 for chlorine is not automatically certified for chloramine, and the listing will say which it covers.
