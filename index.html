SCOPE

What a 24/7 emergency plumber handles in Costa Mesa

An emergency plumber is a plumbing contractor you can reach at any hour to stop active water, sewage or gas problems and make a safe repair. In Costa Mesa, CA, most after-hours calls fall into four groups. A supply line splits under a sink, a garage water heater starts dumping water, the main sewer line backs up into a shower, or a slab leak hisses somewhere under the floor.

We work street addresses in 92626 and 92627, from the Mesa Verde tracts and the South Coast Metro condos down through the Eastside and Westside ranch streets. The city incorporated in 1953, and much of its housing went up in the following decade. That age matters on an emergency call, because original galvanized or early copper supply lines and clay or cast iron drain lines fail in different ways and need different parts on the truck.

Tell the dispatcher the year your home was built if you know it. It helps us bring the right fittings the first time instead of making a second trip.

Your first ten minutes: what to shut off before we arrive

  1. Active leak: close the house shutoff valve. In most Costa Mesa ranch homes it sits where the supply line enters the house, often near the front hose bib. The valve inside the curb meter box belongs to the water utility, so leave that one for Mesa Water or IRWD unless water is flooding the house.
  2. Leaking water heater: turn the gas control to OFF (or switch off the breaker on an electric unit), then close the cold-water valve on top of the tank.
  3. Sewage coming up a drain: stop using every fixture in the house. If your outdoor cleanout cap is easy to reach, loosening it lets the backup spill outside instead of into a bathroom.
  4. Gas smell: leave the house, touch no switches, and call SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 or 911 from outside. A plumber comes after the utility says it is safe.
  5. Take photos of water on floors, walls and ceilings before cleanup starts. Insurers usually ask for them.

Still unsure what to close? Call (714) 750-8637 and we will talk you through it while a plumber heads out.

SERVICE INDEX

Plumbing services we run in Costa Mesa

Six of the most requested jobs. Each links to its own page with causes, method and price factors.

WH

Water heaters

California requires every tank water heater to be braced against earthquakes, and a lot of older garage installs in Costa Mesa were never updated. When a tank leaks from the base, replacing the water heater is usually the honest answer, sized for gas, electric, tankless or heat pump.

DR

Drains

Kitchen lines packed with grease and tub drains slowed by hair are the everyday calls. A clay lateral full of roots is the bigger one, and our drain cleaning and unclogging service handles both with cable machines and water jetting.

LK

Hidden leaks

A water meter that still turns with every tap closed means water is escaping somewhere past the meter. Leak detection uses acoustic listening, thermal imaging and pressure tests to find it before anyone opens a wall.

SW

Sewer lines

The lateral from your house to the district main is yours to maintain, all the way to the connection. Roots, offset joints and bellies show up on camera, and sewer line repair ranges from a spot fix to lining.

CM

The Costa Mesa Sanitary District pays eligible residents back for filming their sewer lateral or adding a cleanout. WATER SUPPLY

What Costa Mesa water does to pipes and water heaters

Mesa Water serves its customers with 100% local groundwater. About 85% comes from shallow wells in the Orange County Groundwater Basin, and about 15% is deep water tinted amber by buried ancient redwoods, which the district clears with nanofiltration before it reaches the tap.

Mesa Water's 2025 Water Quality Report lists 2024 groundwater hardness at an average of 6.4 grains per gallon, with a range from 1.2 to 14. That is moderate on average, and it means scale builds slowly in most homes. The wide range is the reason to test at your own tap before buying a softener, since a single reading from a neighbor tells you little.

The water is disinfected with chloramines. Chloramines are known to wear out some rubber parts faster, so when a toilet runs nonstop, a chloramine-resistant flapper is the part to ask for. Tank water heaters still collect sediment here, and a yearly flush adds years to most of them.
