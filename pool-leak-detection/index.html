Pool Leak Detection & Repair in Costa Mesa, CA

Pool leak detection finds where water is escaping from a pool or spa: the shell, the fittings, the underground lines or the equipment pad. Repair fixes that spot. In Costa Mesa, CA, the first job is proving there is a leak at all, since warm, dry, windy days can pull a surprising amount of water out through evaporation alone.

Run the bucket test first

This simple test tells you whether the pool is leaking or just evaporating.

  1. Set the bucket. Place a bucket on a pool step and fill it to match the pool water level.
  2. Mark both levels. Mark the water line inside the bucket and on the pool wall or tile.
  3. Wait a day. Run the pump on its normal schedule and leave it for 24 hours.
  4. Compare. A pool level that fell clearly further than the bucket level points to a leak.

Repeat the test with the pump off. If the pool loses more with the pump running, the leak is likely on the pressure side, in the return lines. If it loses more with the pump off, look at the suction side or the shell.

Spas and hot tubs leak in the same places, plus the jets and the spillway between spa and pool. A spa that loses water right after a remodel often has a leak at a new fitting or the light niche, and pressure testing the spa lines on their own finds it.

How we pinpoint and repair it

Each line is plugged at the pool and pressurized with air or water. A line that will not hold pressure is leaking, and a listening device on the deck above narrows down where. Fittings, cracks and light niches are checked with dye, which gets drawn toward a leak.

Fitting and crack leaks are often sealed underwater with pool-rated putty or epoxy. A broken underground line means opening the deck at the marked spot, replacing the section, and pressure testing again before the deck is patched. An automatic pool fill line connected to the house supply also needs backflow protection under the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and we check it while we are there.

A wet spot in the yard near the pool is not always the pool. The house water line or irrigation can leak nearby too. Testing the house lines rules that out.

What to do before we come out

  • Run the bucket test and write down the results.
  • Keep the pool filled above the skimmer so the pump does not run dry.
  • Note whether the water drops faster with the pump on or off.
  • Check the equipment pad for wet soil or dripping fittings.

Auto-fill valves and the water bill

Many pools top themselves up through an automatic fill valve connected to the house supply. When the pool leaks, the valve quietly replaces the water, so the level looks normal while the water bill climbs.

Turning the auto-fill off for the bucket test is the only way to see the real loss. If the bill stays high even with the fill off, the leak is probably in the house or yard lines instead.
