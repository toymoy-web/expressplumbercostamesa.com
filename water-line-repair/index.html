Water Line Repair & Replacement in Costa Mesa, CA

Water line repair fixes the buried supply pipe that runs from your water meter to the house. Replacement installs a new one. In Costa Mesa, CA, the meter and everything on the street side belong to the water utility, while the line on your side of the meter is usually the owner's to maintain.

Is the leak yours or the utility's?

The water meter is the dividing line. Where the water shows up tells you who to call first.

Who handles which leak
Where water appearsLikely ownerCall
Bubbling up in the street or gutter.Water utility.Mesa Water or IRWD.
Inside the meter box, at the meter.Water utility.Mesa Water or IRWD.
Soggy strip of yard between meter and house.Property owner, in most cases.A plumber.
At the house shutoff valve.Property owner.A plumber.

Rules differ by utility, so confirm the exact handoff point with yours. Your water bill shows whether you are a Mesa Water or IRWD customer.

Confirm the leak is in the service line

A quick meter test separates a service line leak from a leak inside the house.

  1. Turn off all water use. Faucets, toilets, appliances and irrigation.
  2. Watch the meter. If the small leak indicator moves, water is escaping somewhere.
  3. Close the house shutoff. If the indicator keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the house.

If the indicator stops when the house valve closes, the leak is inside. That is a job for leak detection.

Repair a section or replace the line

When the rest of the pipe is in good shape, we dig at the leak and replace a short piece. Older galvanized service lines are a different story. They tend to fail in several places and restrict flow to the whole house, so replacing the full run usually costs less than repeated digs.

A new line is also the moment to check water pressure. The California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), requires a pressure reducing valve when incoming pressure is over 80 psi. We test it and replace or add the valve if needed, since high pressure shortens the life of every fixture.

Low pressure everywhere in the house, not just one fixture, often traces back to a corroded service line or a failing pressure reducing valve.

What the trench can run into

Service lines in older Costa Mesa yards often share space with sprinkler lines, landscape lighting, gas lines and tree roots. Mature trees are the most common complication, since roots grow along a leaking line toward the water.

Where the ground allows, a new line can be pulled through a narrow bore instead of an open trench, which saves lawns and walkways. Where it does not, we trench and restore the surface. Either way, the new line runs from the meter connection to a new house shutoff valve, so you end up with a shutoff that actually closes.

Before we dig

  • Underground utilities are marked through the 811 call-before-you-dig system.
  • We walk the line path with you to plan around trees, patios and driveways.
  • Water is shut off only while the new line is connected.
  • Irrigation lines crossed during the work are repaired before we backfill.
