Adding an ADU on an Eastside Costa Mesa Lot: The Plumbing Checks to Do First

Written by the Costa Mesa Express Plumbing team. Published September 24, 2026 · Updated October 12, 2026.

Before you design an accessory dwelling unit on an Eastside Costa Mesa lot, check three things: the condition of the existing sewer lateral, the size of the water service line and meter, and how the new unit will get hot water. Many Eastside homes are 1950s and early 1960s ranch houses, and their original lateral and service line were sized for one household. An ADU adds a second kitchen and bathroom to the same pipes.

A camera inspection and a pressure and flow check early in planning cost little and can change the whole design. The same checks apply on the 1960s lots of nearby Fountain Valley.

Why the plumbing check comes first

Eastside lots, many around 6,000 square feet, have room for a detached ADU or a garage conversion, which is why the neighborhood sees so many of them. The structure gets most of the attention in planning. The plumbing often gets looked at only when the trenches are open.

That order is backwards. If the existing lateral is cracked, root-filled or sagging, connecting a new unit to it just doubles the load on a pipe that is already failing. If the water service is undersized, both homes will see weak pressure when two showers run. Knowing this before the plans are final lets you design around it and budget for it.

The sewer lateral: the most common surprise

Costa Mesa makes the lateral the owner's pipe right up to the Costa Mesa Sanitary District's main, which usually runs down the middle of the street. On many Eastside lots, that lateral is original clay or cast iron, and camera inspections often find roots at joints or offset sections.

Filming it before design gives you three options. If the pipe is sound, the ADU can tie in. If it has a few bad joints, repair them as part of the project. If it is failing along its length, replace it once, sized for both units, rather than digging twice.

The Costa Mesa Sanitary District's inspection rebate can help with the camera run for eligible residents. See our guide to sewer camera inspections, and our lateral replacement page for the full-length fix.

Slope matters as much as condition. A detached ADU at the back of a lot needs enough fall to reach the house lateral. A plumber should confirm that before the unit's floor height is set.

What state law says about separate connections

California's ADU law limits when a city or special district can require an ADU to have its own utility connection. In general terms, an ADU created inside the existing home or garage usually cannot be forced to add a separate connection, while a new detached unit can be required to have one, with fees tied to its size or fixture count.

How that applies to your project depends on the unit type and the current rules of the city, the sanitary district and your water utility. Ask each agency early whether a new connection, a larger meter or a capacity fee applies. Those answers affect both the budget and the trench layout.

A plumbing sequence that avoids rework

  1. Film the lateral. Camera the whole run to the main and keep the video.
  2. Test pressure and flow. Measure at a hose bib with fixtures running.
  3. Ask the agencies. Confirm connection and fee rules with the city, CMSD and your water utility.
  4. Design the routes. Plan sewer slope, water, gas and the hot water source for both units.
  5. Fix the main house first. Repair or replace failing pipe before the ADU ties in.
  6. Permit and inspect. Work is inspected under the California Plumbing Code, based on the UPC.

The main house often needs work anyway

Many Eastside homes still have original galvanized supply lines. Tying a new unit into a corroded supply system means both homes share low pressure and future leaks. An ADU project is a natural moment to repipe the main house, since trenches are already open and the water service may be replaced anyway.

If that is on the table, compare costs on our repiping page before the ADU plumbing is finalized. It is cheaper to plan one set of new lines than to replace the old ones a few years later.

Budget reality

The plumbing surprises on ADU projects are usually underground: a failing lateral, a service line that is too small, or a sewer connection that cannot reach the house with proper slope. Every one of those is cheaper to discover with a camera and a gauge than with an excavator. Put the checks at the top of the project, not the bottom.

Costa Mesa note: plan check and impact fees

The City of Costa Mesa reviews ADUs through its standard plan check procedure, and applications go through the TESSA portal. Put the plumbing findings in front of your designer before the plans are drawn, so the sewer, water and gas routes are on the first submittal.

The City's fee sheet, last updated April 20, 2023, says each ADU of 750 square feet or more is subject to impact fees, figured as the ADU's square footage divided by the existing home's, times $13,572. Confirm current fees with the City, and note that Santa Ana and other nearby cities run their own ADU processes.
