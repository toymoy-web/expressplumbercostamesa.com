Swapping to a Heat Pump Water Heater in Costa Mesa: What It Takes and What Rebates Remain in 2026

Written by the Costa Mesa Express Plumbing team. Published September 24, 2026 · Updated September 24, 2026.

A heat pump water heater can work well in a Costa Mesa garage, but in 2026 most homeowners should plan the project without counting on a large rebate. TECH Clean California's main single-family heat pump incentives were nearly fully reserved statewide by November 2025. The income-qualified HEEHRA rebates for single-family homes were fully reserved by February 2026, and new requests now go to a waitlist. The federal heat pump tax credit ended for equipment placed in service after 2025.

Here is what the swap involves, how to check what is still available, and when it still makes sense. The same incentive picture applies in nearby Westminster and across the region.

How a heat pump water heater works

Instead of burning gas or heating an element directly, a heat pump water heater pulls heat from the surrounding air and moves it into the water, much like a refrigerator in reverse. It uses far less electricity than a standard electric tank to heat the same water.

Costa Mesa's mild climate suits it. Garages here rarely get cold enough to slow a heat pump much, and the unit cools and dehumidifies the space around it as a side effect, which many owners like in summer.

Utility and local programs come and go. Before you buy, check the TECH Clean California site and The Switch Is On incentive finder with your address, and ask any installer to show current program terms in writing.

Does it still make sense without a rebate?

It can. If you are replacing an electric resistance tank, a heat pump unit uses much less electricity for the same hot water, so running costs drop noticeably. If you are replacing gas, the savings depend on your gas and electric rates and whether you have rooftop solar.

The upfront cost is the hurdle. Without incentives, a heat pump install usually costs more than a gas tank swap, especially if a new circuit is needed. A like-for-like gas tank remains allowed in Costa Mesa, since the regional air district did not adopt its proposed zero-emission rule in 2025.

Homes with solar panels often get the most out of a heat pump water heater, because it can run on midday solar output.

Questions to answer before you commit

  • Is there space with enough air around the unit, usually in the garage?
  • Does the electrical panel have room for a 240-volt circuit, or is a 120-volt model a fit?
  • Where will the condensate drain go?
  • Are any incentives open for your address today, in writing?
  • Is the current tank failing now, or can you plan the swap calmly?

If your tank is leaking right now, a like-for-like replacement may be the practical move. Our water heater installation page covers gas, electric, tankless and heat pump options.

Permits and code

Costa Mesa homes buy electricity from Southern California Edison and gas from SoCalGas, so any comparison of running costs starts with those two bills. Many garages in Mesa Verde and on the Eastside have the space a heat pump unit needs.

A heat pump install needs a City of Costa Mesa permit, and so does capping the old gas line and sealing the flue. The plumbing side is inspected under the California Plumbing Code (built on the UPC), and the new circuit is inspected under the electrical code.

Capping a gas line is gas work and should be pressure-tested afterward. See our gas line page for what that involves. For a tank that just needs a part, water heater repair may buy you time to plan.

Our honest take

The technology is sound and suits Costa Mesa's climate. The incentive picture in 2026 is thin for most single-family homes. If you want one for efficiency or solar reasons, it is a good choice. If the decision hinges on a rebate, confirm the rebate first.
