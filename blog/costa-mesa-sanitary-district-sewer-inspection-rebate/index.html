How to Claim the Costa Mesa Sanitary District Sewer Inspection Rebate

Written by the Costa Mesa Express Plumbing team. Published September 24, 2026 · Updated October 12, 2026.

The Costa Mesa Sanitary District pays eligible residents back for inspecting their sewer lateral with a camera: up to $200 when the camera goes in through a ground-level cleanout, and up to $250 when it goes in through a roof vent or toilet flange. It also reimburses up to $500 toward installing a permitted ground-level cleanout. The catch is timing and paperwork. You apply first, you get a confirmation number, and the video has to meet specific rules.

This guide walks through who the rebate is for, how to apply, what the video must show, and how to avoid the mistakes that get applications rejected. Next door, Santa Ana handles the street section of a lateral differently.

Why the district pays for your inspection

Here, the lateral belongs to whoever owns the house. It is the pipe that carries wastewater from the house to the public main. That responsibility runs all the way to the connection with the district main, which on most streets sits in the middle of the road. The district owns the main. You own everything upstream of it.

A cracked or root-filled lateral does not only cause backups in your home. It can let groundwater into the sewer system and, in a backup, send sewage into the street. Paying part of the cost of a camera inspection gets more owners to find and fix those problems early, which protects the public system as well.

Rebate amounts at a glance

CMSD Sewer Inspection Rebate Program
What you doMaximum rebateKey condition

Camera inspection from a roof vent or toilet flange.$250.Same video rules apply.
Install a ground-level cleanout.$500.The cleanout must be permitted.

The higher amount for roof vent and toilet flange entry reflects the extra work involved when a home has no cleanout. If your house lacks one, it is worth asking whether adding a cleanout first makes more sense, since the cleanout has its own rebate and makes every future call easier.

How to apply, step by step

Order matters. Most rejected claims come from doing the work before the paperwork.

  1. Apply with the district first. Submit the application to the Costa Mesa Sanitary District before any work is scheduled.
  2. Get your confirmation number. The district issues a confirmation number once the application is accepted. Keep it.
  3. Schedule inside the window. Approval stays valid for three months after the confirmation number, so book the inspection within that time.
  4. Tell the plumber it is for the rebate. The camera setup has to show your address, the date and a footage counter from the start.
  5. Submit the video and invoice. Send the recording and the paid invoice to the district as its instructions describe.

Check the district's current application page before you start. Program details can change, and the district's instructions always take priority over any summary, including this one.

What the video must show

These four items are what reviewers look for. A recording that misses one can be sent back.

  • Your property address, visible on screen.
  • The date of the inspection, visible on screen.
  • A footage counter running for the whole recording.
  • The full lateral from the house until past the connection to the district main.

That last point is the one most often missed. Stopping at the property line or the sidewalk is not enough. The camera has to travel all the way to the main in the street and show the connection.

What the footage tells you, beyond the rebate

The rebate is a nice bonus, but the video is the real value. It shows the pipe material, the condition of every joint, and exactly how far from the entry point any problem sits. In Costa Mesa's older neighborhoods, common findings include roots entering clay joints, cracked or offset sections, grease buildup and low spots where water sits.

If the footage is clean, you have a record to keep for resale. If it shows damage, the repair can be quoted precisely. Our sewer camera inspection page explains what each finding means, and the sewer repair page covers the fixes, from a spot repair to lining.

Homes built in the 1950s and 1960s, common on the Eastside, the Westside and in Mesa Verde, are the most likely to have original clay or cast iron laterals.

When the line is already backed up

If sewage is coming up a drain right now, the rebate is not the first concern. The line needs clearing so the home is usable, which is the job of emergency drain cleaning.

Once the line is flowing, ask for the camera run to be recorded to the district's standard. If you have not yet applied, you can still apply and schedule a separate inspection inside the approval window. It is better to clear the emergency first and film properly later than to rush a recording that gets rejected.

Adding a cleanout while you are at it

Many older homes were built without a ground-level cleanout, the capped pipe outside the house that gives a plumber direct access to the main line. Without one, every service call means pulling a toilet or going in through a roof vent.

The district reimburses up to $500 toward a permitted ground-level cleanout. If your line has needed clearing more than once, a cleanout pays for itself quickly, and the next inspection can use the cleanout entry. The same rule applies here: apply before the work.

Costa Mesa note: applying and getting paid

CMSD takes applications through the online form on its Sewer Inspection Rebate Program page. Approval to take part lasts three months from your confirmation number, so book the camera visit soon after you apply. After the work, you send the district the invoice and proof of payment to be reimbursed.

The program covers residential properties only, not commercial or industrial ones. Questions go to the district at (949) 645-8400. Next door in Irvine, sewer service comes from IRWD, so this rebate does not apply there.
