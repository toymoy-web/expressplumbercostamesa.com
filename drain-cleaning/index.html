One slow drain or a main line backup?

Where the water backs up tells you how deep the clog sits. It also decides how urgent the call is.

Reading the symptoms
What you seeLikely clogUrgency
Only the kitchen sink drains slowly.Grease and food in the kitchen branch line.Same day is fine. Stop using the disposal.
Tub and bathroom sink are slow, toilet is fine.Hair and soap in the bathroom branch.Book a visit.
The toilet gurgles when the washer drains.Partial blockage in the main line.Call soon, before it fully closes.
Water comes up in the shower when you flush.Main sewer line blocked.Emergency. Stop all water use now.

If sewage is already on the floor, stop running every fixture in the house and call. Every flush or shower adds to what backs up.

When one clog shows up in two places

A kitchen sink that backs up into the laundry sink, a washing machine that floods the floor, or an upstairs bath that bubbles up in the kitchen all point the same way. The clog is downstream of where those lines meet, not in either fixture.

Plunging the fixture you can see will not reach it, and chemical cleaners just sit on top of it. Stop running water, including the washer and dishwasher, and call. Water appearing in two places at once is one of the most common reasons people need a plumber at night.

We clear it from the nearest cleanout with a cable sized for that line, then run water from each fixture to prove both drains are moving.

Why Costa Mesa drains clog

Kitchen grease is the most common cause on any street. It goes down warm, cools in the pipe and catches everything that follows. The Costa Mesa Sanitary District takes grease seriously enough to require every restaurant to hold a wastewater discharge permit under its grease control program.

Roots are the second big cause. Many homes built in the 1950s and 1960s still have clay or cast iron laterals, and mature trees in Mesa Verde and on the Eastside send roots into every joint. A cable machine cuts the roots, but they grow back, so a camera look after clearing tells you whether a repair is due.

Wipes sold as flushable, paper towels and cat litter cause the rest. None of them break down the way toilet paper does.

The missing cleanout problem

Plenty of older homes were built without a ground-level cleanout, the capped pipe outside the house that gives a plumber a straight shot at the main line. Without one, we have to pull a toilet or go in through a roof vent, which takes longer and costs more. Today's California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), calls for a cleanout where the building drain leaves the house.

The Costa Mesa Sanitary District encourages adding one and reimburses up to $500 toward a permitted ground-level cleanout. If your home has needed the main line cleared more than once, adding a cleanout usually pays for itself on the next call.

Owners are responsible for the sewer lateral all the way to the district main, including the connection. The main usually runs down the middle of the street, so your pipe is longer than your lot.

How we clear a drain

We match the tool to the line instead of running the biggest machine on every job.

  1. Find the clog. We run water in different fixtures to see which branch is blocked.
  2. Cable it. A hand or drum machine with the right cutter head opens the line.
  3. Jet it when needed. Grease-coated and root-packed lines get water jetting to scour the pipe wall.
  4. Look inside. On a main line, a camera shows roots, cracks or sags so you know if the clog will come back.
  5. Test. We run several fixtures at once to confirm the line drains at full flow.

When a line clogs again within weeks, hydro jetting or a sewer line repair is usually the lasting fix.

Drain emergency? What to do before we arrive

  • Stop using water in every fixture, including the washer and dishwasher.
  • Skip chemical drain openers. They can damage old galvanized and cast iron pipe and burn the plumber who opens the line.
  • If sewage is rising and you can reach the outdoor cleanout cap safely, loosen it so the overflow goes outside.
  • Keep children and pets away from any sewage and photograph the mess for insurance.

Kitchen, bath and laundry lines each clog differently

The fixture tells you what is probably in the pipe, and that decides the tool.

Drain types and typical blockages
LineTypical blockageWhat usually clears it
Kitchen sinkGrease, food and soap building on the pipe wall.Cable, then jetting if it recurs.
Bathroom sink and tubHair and soap scum near the drain.Hand cable or removing the stopper.
ToiletWipes, paper towels or a foreign object.Closet auger.
Laundry standpipeLint and fabric fibers.Cable, plus a lint trap on the hose.
Main lineRoots, grease or a broken section.Drum machine, camera, then repair if needed.

A laundry standpipe that overflows during the rinse cycle is a common call. Washing machines pump water fast, and a line narrowed by lint cannot keep up. A simple mesh lint trap on the discharge hose prevents most of it.

A shower or tub that drains slowly and sometimes backs up into the tub is usually hair and soap at the trap or just past it. If a plunger and a hand tool have not cleared it, a small cable reaches the clog without pulling the drain apart.

Drain cleaning in older Costa Mesa homes

Homes built in the 1950s and 1960s often have cast iron or clay drain lines. Cast iron roughens inside as it ages, which gives grease and paper something to grab. Clay has joints every few feet where roots enter. Both clog more readily than modern plastic pipe.

In these homes, a clog that keeps returning is a signal to film the line rather than keep clearing it. The Costa Mesa Sanitary District's inspection rebate can help with that cost for eligible residents.

Newer plastic drain lines are smoother and clog less, but they still collect grease. The kitchen habits that protect an old line protect a new one too.
