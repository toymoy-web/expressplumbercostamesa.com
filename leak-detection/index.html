Leak Detection in Costa Mesa, CA

Leak detection is the process of pinpointing a hidden water leak inside walls, under a slab or underground, without tearing things open to look. In Costa Mesa, CA, most callers do not know where the water is going. They only know the bill went up, the meter keeps turning, or a floor feels warm.

Our job is to find the exact spot first, so the repair opens one small area instead of a whole room.

The meter keeps moving but you cannot see water anywhere

These are the signs that send most people to a leak test. Any one of them is worth checking.

  • The water bill jumped and nobody in the house changed their habits.
  • The small leak indicator on the meter spins with every tap, toilet and sprinkler off.
  • A warm spot on a tile or concrete floor, often near the kitchen or a bathroom.
  • The sound of running water inside a wall when nothing is on.
  • Damp drywall, lifting baseboards, or a musty smell in one closet or corner.
  • A patch of lawn that stays green and soft when the rest of the yard is dry.

Check your own meter in ten minutes

Before you call anyone, this simple test tells you whether a leak exists and roughly where it is.

  1. Turn everything off. Close every faucet and shower, stop the washer, dishwasher and sprinklers, and make sure no toilet is refilling.
  2. Read the indicator. Open the meter box at the curb and watch the small triangle or dial on the meter face for two minutes.
  3. Close the house valve. Shut the main valve where the line enters your house and watch the meter again.
  4. Read the result. If the indicator stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps turning, the leak is in the line between the meter and the house or in the irrigation system.

Write down what you saw. It saves time on the call and helps us bring the right equipment.

If the bill is what tipped you off, our high-bill check covers the usual causes, and a stain on drywall is covered under ceiling and wall leaks.

How we pinpoint the leak

We start by splitting the plumbing into zones. Closing valves one at a time and watching the meter tells us whether the loss is in the service line, the hot side, the cold side or irrigation.

Then we narrow it down. Ground and floor microphones pick up the hiss of water escaping a pressurized pipe. A thermal camera shows the warm trail of a hot water leak through a slab or wall. On quiet or very slow leaks, a harmless tracer gas pushed into the line rises through the ground or floor, and a sensor finds it.

Pressure testing works the same way inspectors accept new supply piping under the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). A section is isolated, pressurized and watched on a gauge. If the gauge drops, the leak is in that section.

When the spot is confirmed, we mark it and walk you through the repair options before anything is cut.

Hot water leaks under a slab are often easier to find than cold ones. The heat shows up on the thermal camera even when the floor looks dry.

Who owns the leaking pipe

The meter and everything on the street side belong to your water utility. Most of Costa Mesa is served by Mesa Water District, and some addresses by the Irvine Ranch Water District. A leak in the meter box itself or the street is theirs to fix, so call them first if water is bubbling up at the curb.

On the house side of the meter, the pipes are usually the property owner's. Confirm the exact handoff point with your utility, since each agency sets its own rules.

Leak detection for businesses and rentals

Commercial spaces and multi-unit buildings add complications. Supply lines serve several units or restrooms, fixtures cannot always be shut off during business hours, and the leak may be in a ceiling above an occupied space.

We isolate the building by zone, test after hours when water can be shut down, and give the owner or property manager written findings. That record matters when a tenant, a neighbor and an insurer all need the same answer about where the water came from.
