A dripping relief valve is a warning, not just a leak

The temperature and pressure relief valve opens when pressure or temperature inside the tank climbs too high. If it drips, the valve may be worn, or it may be doing its job because pressure is too high.

The California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), calls for a pressure reducing valve when street pressure is over 80 psi. Homes with a reducing valve or a check valve also need an expansion tank, because heated water has nowhere to go. We test the pressure before replacing the relief valve, so the new one does not start dripping next week.

Never cap or plug a dripping relief valve. It is the part that keeps a tank from failing violently.

Safe checks before you call

These take a few minutes and sometimes solve the problem outright.

  • Electric tank: check the breaker. If it tripped, reset it once. If it trips again, leave it off.
  • Gas tank: look through the sight glass for the pilot flame. Relight only by following the label on the tank.
  • Smell gas at any point: stop, leave the garage, and call SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200.
  • Leak at the base: close the cold-water valve on top and turn the gas control to OFF.

Local water and what wears out first

Mesa Water's 2025 report lists 2024 groundwater hardness at an average of 6.4 grains per gallon. That is moderate, so sediment builds slowly, but it still settles on the tank bottom and makes a gas tank rumble and overheat its base over time. A flush during a repair visit is often worth adding.

The water is disinfected with chloramines. Some rubber washers and seals on drain valves and connectors wear faster under chloramine, which is one reason leaking drain valves are a common call.
