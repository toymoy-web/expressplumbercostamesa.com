Who handles what in Irvine

Irvine is simpler than most of Orange County: one district runs the water, the sewer and the recycled water. The City of Irvine handles plumbing permits.

IRWD was formed in 1961 and covers about 181 square miles, all of Irvine plus parts of neighboring cities. Everything on your side of the meter and your own sewer lateral is a job for a plumber.

Purple pipes: the recycled water rule

Much of Irvine's landscape irrigation runs on recycled water from IRWD, carried in purple pipe and marked with purple valve boxes and sprinkler heads. It is treated for irrigation and other approved uses, not for drinking.

That matters on plumbing jobs. A recycled water line must never be connected to a home's drinking water piping, and any work near one needs care so the two systems stay separate. If a purple line is leaking in a common area or along a street, the HOA or IRWD handles it. If a drinking water line is leaking beside it, that line is on your side and ours to fix.

Never use a hose bib or valve marked as recycled water for drinking, filling a pool or washing food.

Condos, HOAs and newer homes

A large share of Irvine homes sit inside homeowner associations. Before any work in a condo or townhome, check whether the leaking line belongs to your unit or the association, since the governing documents decide who pays.

Newer homes in the Great Park and Portola Springs areas often use PEX supply lines with a manifold and tankless water heaters. If the home is still under a builder warranty, call the builder first for covered defects. For an active leak at night, stop the water and call us, then sort out the warranty in the morning.

Older Woodbridge and Northwood homes on slabs are the likeliest to need slab leak work or a whole-house repipe.
