The whole sewer lateral is yours

Santa Ana owns and maintains roughly 390 miles of public sewer main serving more than 45,000 laterals. A 2015 ordinance made the long-standing rule official: the property owner owns the entire lateral, from the house to and including the connection at the main, including the part under the street.

There is one useful exception in practice. Since 2001, the city's sewer enterprise may repair or replace the lower lateral in the public right-of-way at the Public Works director's discretion when funds are available. Before paying for street work, call Public Works and ask whether your repair qualifies.

If sewage is overflowing from a manhole in the street, that is the city's main. Call Santa Ana Public Works. If only your home is backing up, call a plumber.

Santa Ana Burst Pipe or Active Leak: What to Do Before We Arrive

Close the old valve gently.

Historic Santa Ana homes often have an original gate valve near the front hose bib. Turn it slowly and stop if it will not budge, then use the meter valve.

Power off where it is wet.

Switch off breakers for rooms where water reaches outlets, and stay clear of old wiring in damp areas.

Stop all water use for a backup.

If sewage is rising, every flush or shower adds to it. If a cleanout is easy to reach, loosening its cap lets the overflow go outside.

Older pipes in the historic districts

Homes in French Park, Washington Square and Floral Park often still have galvanized steel supply lines and clay or cast iron drains. Galvanized pipe rusts from the inside until pressure drops and a thin spot leaks. Clay laterals let roots in at every joint.

A camera run before a sewer repair, and a pressure check before patching another supply leak, keep you from paying twice. See recorded sewer camera inspections and whole-house repiping for how those jobs work.

If a repair will change the outside of a home in a historic district, check with the city first, since exterior changes there can need extra review.

Water service in Santa Ana

Most Santa Ana homes buy water from the city's own water utility, so the water bill names the city. Leaks at the meter or in the street belong to the utility. Leaks on the house side of the meter are the owner's.

A small number of addresses near the borders are served by neighboring districts. If in doubt, the name on the bill settles it.
