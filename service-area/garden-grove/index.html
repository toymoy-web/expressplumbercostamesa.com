The sewer lateral rule in Garden Grove

The Garden Grove Sanitary District is responsible for the city's public sewer, which the city's Water Services Division maintains: more than 312 miles of sewer line and about 9,700 manholes. Property owners are responsible for maintaining, repairing and cleaning the lateral from the house to the public sewer.

Since 2008, a district ordinance has required owners to camera inspect their lateral after a sewage overflow that reaches public property or a neighbor's property, and to repair or replace it if the inspection finds problems. A backup that stays inside your home is still urgent, but one that spills into the street or next door can come with a required inspection.

For a public sewer spill, such as a manhole overflowing in the street, the city's Public Works office is the contact. For your own lateral, call a plumber.

If your Garden Grove sewer backs up

  1. Stop the water. Stop using every fixture so the backup does not grow.
  2. Contain it. If sewage is leaving your property, keep it out of the gutter and storm drain if you safely can.
  3. Clear the line. We clear the lateral so the home is usable.
  4. Record the camera run. The video documents the cause and is what an inspection requirement asks for.
  5. Repair if needed. Cracks, roots or offsets are repaired or the lateral replaced.

See sewer camera inspection and sewer line replacement for how those steps work.

Preventing the next overflow

Most lateral overflows in older neighborhoods come from roots at the joints or grease in the line. Once the line is cleared and filmed, the footage tells you whether routine cleaning is enough or whether a joint needs repair before it lets roots back in.

A ground-level cleanout outside the house makes every future call faster and lets a backup spill outside instead of into a bathroom. If your home lacks one, adding it during a repair is usually the right time.
