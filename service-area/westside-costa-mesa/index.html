The Westside is where homes, workshops and small businesses share the same blocks. The City's General Plan calls it an intertwined mix of industrial, commercial and residential uses, and it is one of the residential neighborhoods the plan names.

Homes beside the city's oldest industrial area

Westside history starts early. The Costa Mesa Historical Society records the Newport-Mesa Tract, south of 19th Street, in 1907 and the Fairview Farms tract between 19th Street and Wilson Street in 1913. Lions Park on West 18th Street opened as Costa Mesa Park in 1940.

The General Plan describes the Westside District as the City's oldest industrial area, about 312 acres. In 2005 the City added mixed-use overlay zones to the Westside urban plans, and the Mesa West Residential Ownership plan aims to add homes people can own.

So the housing here ranges from older houses on long, narrow lots to new townhomes and live-work buildings. We do not assume what pipe any one of them has. We check.

Backflow for Westside businesses

Many Westside businesses have a backflow prevention assembly, and Mesa Water runs its testing program through the SpryBackflow portal. Testers must be certified and approved by Mesa Water in that portal before they can submit results.

Our backflow testing page explains the annual test, repairs when a device fails, and how the results are filed.

Water heaters and drains in Westside homes

In single-family homes and townhomes, the water heater is usually in the garage or a closet. If it leaks, close the cold inlet, shut off the gas or breaker and call. Water heater repair covers what can be fixed and when replacement makes sense.

If more than one drain backs up at once, stop using water. That points to a blockage downstream, often in the lateral, and it needs clearing before anyone showers or runs the washer.

When you call about a Westside property
