How the seasons change plumbing calls in Laguna Beach

Winter storms bring the hillside risks. Heavy rain can find its way into cracked sewer laterals and overload them, and saturated slopes put stress on buried pipe. The city has seen that before: the Bluebird Canyon landslide in June 2005 followed a very wet winter. If a sewer line on a steep lot backs up during or after a storm, have it filmed, since ground movement can open joints.

Summer brings the crowds. Visitor season and full vacation rentals push more water through drains, sewer laterals and water heaters than those systems see the rest of the year. Rentals that sit empty through winter and then fill up are prone to running toilets, dried-out seals and slow drains in the first busy weeks.

Spring and fall are the best time for planned work: a camera run under the city's lateral incentive program, a pressure regulator check, or a water heater replacement before the summer rush.

Water pressure on a hillside

The Laguna Beach County Water District moves water through five elevation zones using 14 pumping stations and 21 reservoirs. A home low on a canyon road and one near a ridge can see very different pressure from the same system.

High pressure wears out fixtures, water heater relief valves and supply connectors. Low pressure makes showers weak and can point to a failing regulator. On any hillside call, we put a gauge on the line first. The California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), calls for a pressure reducing valve when incoming pressure is above 80 psi.

South Laguna homes are served by the South Coast Water District instead. Your water bill shows which one.

Burst Pipe or Active Leak in Laguna Beach: What to Do Before We Arrive

Close the main valve.

Hillside homes often have the shutoff near the street or in a box at the top of the driveway, since the service line climbs up from the street. Find it before you need it.

Kill power to wet areas.

Switch off breakers for any room where water reaches outlets, and stay clear of wet electrical in older cottages with original wiring.

Watch the slope.

Water from a burst line on a steep lot can wash out soil or run onto a neighbor. Note where it is flowing and keep people off soaked slopes.
