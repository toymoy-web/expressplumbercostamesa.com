Plumber in Eastside Costa Mesa, CA

The Eastside is one of the oldest residential parts of Costa Mesa. The Costa Mesa Historical Society dates it to 1906, when 1,700 acres of what was then called Newport Heights were subdivided, almost fifty years before the city incorporated in 1953.

Today the City's General Plan describes the Eastside as a place of infill: older houses on long, narrow lots, rebuilds, ADUs and new small-lot homes. Each of those changes what a plumber needs to check. Call (714) 750-8637 and tell us whether the house is original, remodeled or new.

Older lots, newer homes

The City's General Plan says many homes were built before incorporation, particularly on the Eastside, and that those early subdivisions used long, narrow lots. It also says most new single-family construction in the city is infill, particularly on the Eastside and Westside.

In practice that means a single block can hold a house from before 1953, a full rebuild and a new small-lot home. The City approved new small-lot subdivisions in Eastside multi-family zones between 2012 and 2016. The plumbing in each can be from a different decade.

We do not assume an older house has any particular pipe. Some have been repiped, some have not, and a short inspection tells you which before you plan a repair.

Long lots and the sewer lateral

The sewer lateral runs from the house to the Costa Mesa Sanitary District main under the street, and the owner is responsible for all of it. On a long lot with the house set back, that run can be longer than on a typical tract lot, so the camera is the fastest way to see its condition.

If the house has no cleanout near the front, the district will reimburse part of adding one, up to $500 with a permit, and it has a separate rebate for filming a residential lateral. A front cleanout also makes every future drain call faster.

See sewer camera inspection for what the video shows, and sewer line repair if the camera finds a break.

Adding an ADU or rebuilding on the Eastside

An ADU or a rebuild adds fixtures to the same lot, so the plumbing questions come before the drawings.

The City reviews ADUs through its standard plan check, with applications through the TESSA portal. Its April 2023 fee sheet applies impact fees to ADUs of 750 square feet or more. Confirm current fees with the City before you budget.

Remodels, repipes and water heaters

When an older Eastside house is opened up for a kitchen or bath remodel, it is the cheapest moment to replace old supply lines. Our repiping page explains copper and PEX, and what a whole-house job involves.

Water heaters here sit in garages, closets and sometimes new ADUs. Water heater installation covers sizing for a house plus a second unit.
