Tankless Water Heater Service in Costa Mesa, CA

Tankless water heater service covers descaling, error-code diagnosis, repair and installation of on-demand units that heat water only when a tap opens. In Costa Mesa, CA, a flush every year or two keeps most units running at full output.

Signs your tankless unit needs attention

  • Hot water that turns cold in the middle of a shower, then comes back.
  • An error code on the display, often related to ignition, flow or overheating.
  • Lower hot water flow than when the unit was new.
  • The unit has not been flushed in more than two years.
  • A whistling or rattling sound from the unit while it fires.

Why descaling matters here

A tankless unit pushes water through a narrow heat exchanger. Mineral scale builds on those passages and cuts both flow and efficiency, and many units flag it with an error before they fail.

Mesa Water's 2025 report puts its 2024 groundwater hardness at an average of 6.4 grains per gallon, with a range from 1.2 to 14. At the moderate end, a flush every other year is often enough. Homes that see the harder end of the range, or run the unit hard, do better with a yearly flush.

A descaling visit circulates a mild descaling solution through the heat exchanger with isolation valves, then flushes it clean and cleans the inlet screen.

No isolation valves on your unit? We can add them during the first service, which makes every future flush quicker and cheaper.

What a new tankless install involves

Most gas tankless units need a larger gas supply than the old tank. Many 1950s and 1960s Costa Mesa homes have gas lines sized for a tank and a furnace, so the line often has to be upsized from the meter.

Venting changes too. Condensing units vent through plastic pipe and need a drain for condensate. Non-condensing units need stainless steel venting. Venting follows the manufacturer instructions and the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Every install needs a City of Costa Mesa permit.

Gas line work is covered in more detail on our gas line installation and repair page.

Tankless units show up often in remodeled Eastside homes and in newer townhomes on the Westside and around South Coast Metro, where garage and closet space is tight.

Common error code groups

Brands use different numbers, but the causes fall into a few groups.

  • Ignition failure: often gas supply, a dirty burner or a weak igniter.
  • Flame loss mid-cycle: low gas pressure or venting problems.
  • Overheat or scale warnings: usually a heat exchanger that needs descaling.
  • Low flow: a clogged inlet screen or a failing flow sensor.
  • Condensate or drain errors on condensing units: a blocked condensate line.

Read the code and the model number to the dispatcher. It often narrows the part before we arrive.
