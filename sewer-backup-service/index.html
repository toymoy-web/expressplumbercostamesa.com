Emergency Sewer Backup Service in Costa Mesa, CA

Sewer backup service gets a backed-up main line flowing again, finds out why it backed up, and stops it happening again. In Costa Mesa, CA, the blockage is almost always in the homeowner's lateral, which the owner maintains all the way to the district main.

We answer around the clock, because a sewer backup cannot wait for business hours.

What happens when we arrive

The first job is flow. We open the line from a ground-level cleanout if the home has one. Without one, we go in through a toilet flange or a roof vent. A drum machine sized for the main line cuts through the blockage.

Once the house drains, we run a camera through the line. It shows whether the cause was a one-time clog or something that will return: roots at a joint, a sag that collects waste, or a broken section.

After the line is clear: cleanup

Sewage is the dirtiest kind of water damage. The industry's IICRC S500 standard treats it as Category 3, so carpet, pad and the lower part of soaked drywall are usually removed rather than dried in place.

Small spills on tile can be cleaned carefully with gloves and disinfectant. Anything larger is a job for a restoration crew, reached on a separate line.

Stop using all water in the house the moment sewage appears. Every flush and shower adds to what backs up.

Preventing the next one

If the home has no ground-level cleanout, adding one is the single most useful upgrade. It lets a future backup spill outside instead of into a bathroom, and it gives the next plumber direct access. The Costa Mesa Sanitary District offers a rebate toward installing one.

Homes with repeated backups can also ask about a backwater valve, which lets wastewater out but helps stop it flowing back in. Whether one fits depends on the home's layout, and it needs a permit. Work is inspected under the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

Backups in condos and apartment buildings

In multi-unit buildings, several homes often share one lateral. A blockage there backs up into the lowest units first, even when those residents did nothing to cause it.

We clear the shared line, film it, and document where the blockage sat, which helps the association or owner decide responsibility. Scheduled cleaning is common for buildings where grease or wipes keep causing trouble.

What to do before we arrive

  • Stop using every fixture, including the washer, dishwasher and showers.
  • Keep children and pets away from the sewage.
  • If the outdoor cleanout cap is easy and safe to reach, loosen it so the overflow goes outside.
  • Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and safe to reach.
  • Photograph the backup and damage before cleanup starts.
