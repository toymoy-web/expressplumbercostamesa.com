Emergency Burst Pipe Repair in Costa Mesa, CA

Burst pipe repair stops water escaping from a split, cracked or separated supply line and replaces the damaged section. In Costa Mesa, CA, pipes rarely freeze, so bursts usually come from corrosion, high water pressure or a failed connector under a sink or behind an appliance.

What to do before we arrive

Every minute of spraying water adds to the damage. These steps come before anything else.

  1. Close the house shutoff. Usually where the supply line enters the house, often near the front hose bib.
  2. Kill power near the water. Switch off breakers for any outlets or appliances in the wet area if you can reach the panel safely.
  3. Open a low faucet. An outdoor hose bib drains pressure out of the lines.
  4. Move valuables and take photos. Document the damage before cleanup, for insurance.

If the house valve will not close, the curb valve in the meter box belongs to the water utility. In an emergency, call Mesa Water or IRWD, or tell us and we will talk you through it.

Freezing is rare here. These are the usual causes behind a Costa Mesa burst.

Check the pressure, or it will happen again

The California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), requires a pressure reducing valve when incoming pressure is above 80 psi. Reducing valves wear out over time, and when one fails, pressure in the house climbs to street pressure.

We put a gauge on a hose bib on every burst call. If pressure is high, fixing the pipe without fixing the valve just moves the next failure somewhere else.

Repeated bursts in an older home usually mean the pipe itself is done. A full repipe replaces it all at once instead of one emergency at a time.

Washing machine hoses are a common burst point. Braided steel hoses, replaced every five years or so, are cheap insurance.

Condos and shared walls

In a condo or townhome, a burst line may sit inside a shared wall or feed from a common riser. The unit shutoff may not stop it, and the association may control the building valve.

Call the HOA's emergency number at the same time you call us. Tell us the building and unit, and whether water is reaching a neighbor. Who pays usually depends on whether the failed pipe served only your unit, which is something our repair notes can document for the association.

After the leak stops: drying out

Fixing the pipe does not dry the wall or subfloor behind it. Wet drywall, insulation and cabinets need proper drying to avoid mold.

Water damage cleanup and water mitigation are handled by a separate dedicated line, listed on those pages. Call them once the water is off.
